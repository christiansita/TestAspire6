# AI Content Brain Constitution

## Core Principles

### I. Creator-First Ideation
AI Content Brain exists to turn vague ideas, audience signals, and channel constraints into concrete, production-ready content plans. Every idea must begin with a defined creator goal, target audience, and success metric before scripts, hooks, or campaigns are drafted. The system must reduce blank-page friction and move from uncertainty to clarity without sacrificing strategic intent.

### II. Platform-Centered Execution
Content is never generic. Every brief, hook, script, caption, or campaign must be shaped for the platform where it will live. We must respect audience behavior, format limits, attention curves, and production realities across YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, podcasts, and blogs. The same core message may be adapted across channels, but each execution must be intentionally designed for that environment.

### III. Data-Aware Decision Making
Ideas must be informed by observed performance, historical engagement, and audience behavior. We do not create content in a vacuum. We ground recommendations in prior results, retention signals, and channel-specific patterns. When evidence conflicts with intuition, data must guide the decision unless a deliberately documented strategic exception is required.

### IV. Consistency and Brand Clarity
We must preserve a consistent voice, narrative arc, and quality standard across all formats. Brand identity, audience promise, and message hierarchy must remain recognizable regardless of channel. Every draft must align to the creator's strategic positioning and remain usable by a creator or team without additional heavy rework.

### V. Production-Ready Output
The system's output must be actionable and publishable without requiring major cleanup. A strong result is a brief, script, hook, angle, or campaign plan a creator can use immediately. We avoid vague recommendations, generic brainstorms, and low-conviction outputs when a more specific, higher-value option is possible.

## Content Standards

Content work must begin with a clear understanding of the audience, channel, objective, and content type. Each recommendation must identify the intended audience, the core promise, the desired outcome, and the production format. Hooks, scripts, and captions must reflect platform-specific attention patterns, tone expectations, and pacing constraints.

We must maintain quality gates across every workflow: clarity, relevance, audience fit, originality, and execution readiness. Outputs should be structured, publishable, and strategically useful. Repeatable systems are preferred over one-off creativity when they preserve quality and speed.

## Operating Model

The working model is: research first, decide second, execute third. We convert vague concepts into structured outputs such as angle, hook, structure, deliverable, and production notes. Content planning must prioritize rapid iteration from idea to draft to publish-ready material while preserving strategic coherence and brand consistency.

We must use historical performance and audience signals to guide creative decisions, optimize next steps, and reduce wasted production effort. If a creative choice is ambiguous or strategic tradeoffs are unclear, the creator or team must review the decision before the output is treated as final.

## Governance

This constitution defines the non-negotiable standard for AI Content Brain. It supersedes ad hoc ideation practices, generic AI prompting, and inconsistent content planning. Changes to these principles require documentation, rationale, and explicit review before they become active policy.

All content outputs must be checked for audience fit, platform suitability, strategic clarity, and production readiness. Any work that fails to serve the creator's objective or ignores platform realities is non-compliant. Governance is satisfied when content is reviewed against these principles before it is considered ready to publish.
